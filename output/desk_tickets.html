Expected What we expect the team to do

Lead

Boss should call the vendor first, to see if they are expecting new inventory of the shirt the customer wants to order, before cancelling the order and losing potential income.

Delegation flow

  1. Boss reads the ticket and sees the size S tee is out of stock.
  2. Boss delegates to the vendor to flag what inventory is coming.
  3. The vendor update shows whether the shop can expect to carry the item being ordered.
  4. Only after that does the Boss decide whether to keep or cancel the order.

MCP tools likely used

How this maps to our harness
  • No vendor calls (rule 8): there is no vendor agent, and real vendors are never contacted. "Calling the vendor" would be Inventory using list_vendors (lead time, open invoices) and request_restock, which posts a draft to the board.
  • Unpaid invoice (rule 3): Bulldog Print Co won't ship while invoice #501 is open, so request_restock will be refused until Accounting pays it with pay_invoice, which needs human approval.

Resolved Size S tee was out of stock and the vendor was blocked by overdue invoice 501. After a human approved paying the $840 invoice and an $8 purchase order for 1 tee, Bulldog Print Co can ship (5-day lead time, expected 2026-09-05). The customer has a draft reply with that date, so the Boss resolved the ticket.

  4. Runs 4–5: the Boss re-checked with Inventory, Accounting and Customer Service, and kept the ticket in progress while it waited for delivery.
  5. Run 6: the Boss alone read the ticket and board, saw the paid restock and the customer's arrival date (2026-09-05), and set the ticket to resolved.

Expected What we expect the team to do

Lead

Boss will call Facilities to track the rent notice and plan for payment, since rent is due in 2 days.

Delegation flow

  1. Boss delegates the ticket to Facilities.
  2. Facilities tracks the rent notice and plans the payment.
  3. Facilities delegates to Accounting, which tracks cash inflow and outflow for the rent and makes sure the store's financial statements are accurate.

MCP tools likely used

  • No inflow (rule 6): revenue isn't modeled, so cash only goes out. Paying rent leaves $1,000, or $160 if invoice #501 is also paid.

Reflection

Problem 10 · Looking back at the Expected plans (Problem 6) and the Actual run (Problem 9)

1. Overall performance

Overall, the agent team performed well. It was clearly checking every step along the way. When the agents' tools couldn't finish a ticket (there was no way to approve the bulk discount for ticket 103), that gap was found and a new tool (prepare_discount) was added, so the ticket could be resolved. The agents also adapted within the rules: Accounting offered the discount only on the 8 hoodies in stock instead of ordering a restock the shop couldn't afford. The agents also clearly followed the instruction to verify that the math and the flow of cash were correct.

2. Expected vs. Actual

I was surprised that my Expected plans missed many "sub-steps". The Actual run revealed a larger web of approvals than I expected.

For example, I didn't expect Accounting to be involved in ticket 101 to pay an invoice. I assumed tracking the expected shipment from the vendor was enough.

3. One agent vs. a team

One agent could have been simpler, because there wouldn't be as much movement and tracking across all the different agents. Tools would allow more consolidated action, and possibly more efficiency in resolving open tickets.

4. Future issues where agents could help Campus Customs

Backorders and preordersAgents can track the expected arrival and handle communication with customers about when items will ship, once payment has already been made.
Acquiring a new location's leaseAgents can help track how a new lease affects cash flow, and where cash is being redirected (for example, to the new lease).
Returns and receiving stock backAgents can help track the inflow of previously sold items, and how this affects the financial statements through Accounting.

5. What the agents can't support

  • Customer profiles and reorders: agents can't remember customer profiles or support reorders based on each customer's interests.
  • Processing returns: processing a return itself is beyond what the agents can do today.
  • Deal management: agents can't manage deals for selling property or equipment the store needs.
  • Facilities' scope: Facilities seemed limited to the rent side of the business and keeping it running.
